# NovaPump Reliability Review — Synthetic Case Study

## The engineering question

Is the available reliability evidence sufficient to bring the NovaPump configuration to an accountable engineering review?

This synthetic case illustrates a familiar regulated-product-development challenge: evidence exists across tests, reviews, and technical records, but the team needs a transparent view of what is complete, what conflicts, and what still requires human attention.

## Context

| Dimension | Demonstration context |
|---|---|
| Product | NovaPump infusion pump platform |
| Review focus | Reliability readiness for a high-use configuration |
| Key stakeholders | Systems, Mechanical, Quality, and V&V |
| Decision boundary | Review readiness only—not design, production, or regulatory approval |

## Evidence picture

- 86% coverage across the defined engineering categories
- 70% structural consistency, with two observations requiring documented rationale
- One high-priority reliability evidence gap: environmental-cycle evidence for the highest-use configuration
- One material-life assumption requiring a documented basis

## Deterministic readiness view

The demonstration combines available **coverage**, **consistency**, **validation**, and **confidence** inputs into an informational Engineering Completeness Index of **82/100**.

The resulting status is:

> **Needs Engineering Review**

That status is intentionally not an approval. It tells the review team that the evidence is sufficient to focus a human review on named limitations and open items.

## Value demonstrated

- Makes evidence gaps visible before a formal review.
- Preserves the distinction between facts, assumptions, limitations, and human judgment.
- Gives cross-functional stakeholders a shared, traceable review agenda.
- Avoids opaque scoring or automated authorization.

## Boundary

All names, evidence records, metrics, and findings in this case study are synthetic. This project does not include client, employer, clinical, or proprietary data.
